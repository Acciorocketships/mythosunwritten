# Irregular green mountain shoulders

Reference: the retained seven-camera mountain study, especially `hero`; P04 is
the production context. Study cameras are preserved, not reconstructed from P04.

The former generator passed its detailed outline through a convex hull before
fracturing. That removed concave terrace shoulders. The separate horizontal
shelves did not widen their host column down to ground.

Alternatives considered:

- More shelf props: easy to place but repeats the unsupported slab problem.
- Separate descending columns: rooted, but recreates the small stacked-pyramid
  appearance and leaves most of the face unchanged.
- A shared stepped profile applied after fracturing: retains the real stone
  gaps, creates plantable shoulders, and increases basal width continuously.
  Selected for the source asset study. Production integration must still derive
  its boundaries from the existing exposed grid faces.

Two geometry regressions measure actual exposed upward triangles (excluding
buried fracture faces) in three intermediate height bands and lower/upper depth.
The original source fails nine asset-specific checks. The original closed-solid,
normal, finite-value, deterministic and 15,000-triangle limits remain in force.

Candidate 1 introduced sub-centimetre sliver edges and exceeded one mesh budget.
Rejected. Candidate 2 collapses only those tiny shared edges and spaces the
primary fracture seeds at 6.8 m, preserving the original bevel operation. All
four source tests pass. Visual review is pending.
