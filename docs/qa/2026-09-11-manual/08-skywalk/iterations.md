# Skywalk integration — accepted visual/construction repair

Baseline: accepted issue 6/7 geometry, saved as `before-payload.bin`; live baseline
is `../06-variety/live-after`. The source has two occupied spans. The first span
and its two endpoint rooms form a separate three-room mass above one-band gaps.
One lower endpoint is itself a separate two-room column. The other span belongs
to the main 22-room group. There is also one deliberately freestanding house.

The single planter is a generic flat-crown accent in one of those low gaps,
not a skywalk landing or garden with access. The fixed timber corner frames make
the upper construction supported, but leave its mass visibly detached.

Candidate approaches: fill completely borne half-storey gaps with native city
masonry; move spans to whole-storey datums; or add new stair/gallery connections.
Moving these spans would disturb the clearance over their existing streets.
Filling the two existing one-band gaps can join both occupied spans and all 27
rooms of the warren into one continuous building group without inventing a new
unusable passage. The separate small house can remain freestanding.

Implement a finite native masonry course only when its whole footprint rests
on actual inhabited rooms, carries an actual upper root, and occupies no public
or protected air. Reserve that course before choosing roof decorations so the
planter is never placed there. Keep ordinary tall/open frames where a complete
course is not supported. Verify native wall/slab contacts and visual seams,
whole-warren connectivity, unchanged public walking, and all 48 corpus towns.
This is construction connectivity; it does not invent traversable interiors for
closed room assets.


## Rejected candidates

1. The first admission proof produced no courses. It consulted room-only ground
   connectivity before completed foundations existed and rejected the actual
   outside-tagged roof-reserved band. Admission now checks complete upper/lower
   room footprints and protected air early; emission proves lower bearing against
   the completed foundation graph.
2. Two courses joined the graph and passed centre-line contacts, but close
   underside views exposed openings. Rejected; `candidate2-*` artifacts retained.
3. Restoring the old measured timber corner posts passed the original tower
   radial check, but the same underside view still showed openings. All-footprint
   checks also failed. The real cause was the narrow 1.770 m stone panel repeated
   on a 3 m interval. A ray from a central point could hit another wall behind a
   near opening and falsely credit coverage. Rejected; `candidate3-*` retained.
4. Current candidate uses the complete native 3.085 m wall on its normal 3 m
   repeat, with the existing native miter alternatives at perpendicular joins.
   The timber corner members continue the upper and lower facades. Checks use
   absolute heights inside the native course, every footprint, and off-centre
   cross-sections. Close renders must pass before the final matrix is run.


Candidate 4 passes all final images (12 live, 16 native, 10 unobscured detail
pairs; two obstructed detail views excluded), 10 tests / 266 assertions, and
unchanged 88-cell/124-crossing local collision. All 48 towns construct with
11,112 clear public positions and 15,923 clear crossings. Composition has only
the 3/standard timing failure, 17,856 ms vs 16,200 at invalid host factor 3.35x.
Timing remains unresolved and pins stay unchanged. This accepts issue 8's
construction/visual scope. The next item is prefab integration.
