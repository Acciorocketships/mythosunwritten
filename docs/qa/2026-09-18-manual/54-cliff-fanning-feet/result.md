# Lateral shelf-bearing study — not promoted

Production remains the larger-section pass 51. This study follows the rejected height-only boulder experiment in pass 52. Current ledge supports retain essentially the same lateral footprint down the wall. The new fixture samples adjacent authored shelf profiles and spreads their bearing sideways with descent, retaining the local support and the current full-height upper-projection envelope.

## Red-first geometry

The isolated 16 m shelf has no lower support four metres outside its footprint on production; the new support provides 1.950985 m there and zero near the upper rim. Its widening remains continuous and does not recess toward the base. The production red run fails one of four assertions.

The first maximum-union trial (`fanned`) broadens feet but fills some smaller shelf treads. `bearing` translates both tread edges together to retain those ledges. That prototype introduces a 1.599431 m support discontinuity across a ledge elevation. A separate failing regression pins it. `continuous` replaces the hard height admission with a smooth transition and reduces that jump to 0.000565 m. Both support tests then pass five assertions.

The final continuous-shape run passes eight tests / seventeen assertions before the extra discontinuity test was added. It retains 4.75–40.75 m connected upper shelf spans and zero excess over the gradual upper-silhouette envelope. The broad-outline correlation improves from production 0.527707 to 0.462818. These runs overlap; their counts should not be summed as independent tests.

The earlier `bearing` fixture passes eighteen integration tests / 96 assertions covering grass, corners, root tangency and ledge channels. It retains 450 supported grass patches and closed 16/32/64 m corner controls. This is explicitly evidence for the intermediate fixture, not an integration certification of the later variants.

## Native judgment

The initial five `tall` captures use Mobile rendering and lack the reference's SSAO; they are exploratory and not the matched lighting comparison. Five `bearing-tall` and five `continuous-tall` images use Forward+ with the reference studio setup. The bearing oblique/ledge views expose the new sharp tread-edge artifacts; the continuous ledge view removes those abrupt edges.

Seventeen `world` captures rebuild the intermediate bearing geometry at saved yellow-region anchors. The inspected P20 view remains visually very close to production. This is frozen terrain/grass context, not a fresh world or traversal check. The donor spacing begins at 4 m, so its widening has little effect on the photographed 4–8 m cliffs; a successful tall control is not evidence of improvement at that ordinary scale.

The widened feet improve the numerical outline measure but still leave long upright faces and a rather straight, broad lower silhouette. They do not yet deliver the requested varied, rocky terraced slopes, so no variant is promoted.

A final `combined` experiment applies the pass-52 physical-width boulder-height bound to the continuous support. Five tests / eleven assertions pass; upper spans are 2–42.25 m. Its outline correlation worsens to 0.573720. Five Forward+ views are retained; the inspected oblique view still shows upright blocks and a broad flattened foot. Limiting boulder height and broadening support together still do not establish the desired composition.

The next shape change needs to alter the actual large rock volumes and their joins at the photographed ordinary heights. Merely filling the gaps between existing upright shelf supports does not solve that silhouette. Production code, materials and water remain unchanged. No general art, collision, performance or original-register acceptance is claimed.
