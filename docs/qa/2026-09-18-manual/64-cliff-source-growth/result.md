# Rock body and support studies — not promoted

This study keeps pass 60's source as `before.gd`. It investigates the interaction between inherited shelf supports, Native Nature rock profiles, and the upper projection limit. No prototype is promoted. The user's later instruction to remove crags supersedes the surface-detail direction of these experiments; that work continues in pass 66.

`grown` weights supports by descent before constructing treads, but loses turf and upper shelf area. `seated` begins the independent body at the actual wall surface, without solving those problems. `carved` cuts above ledges and gives shallow adjacent shoulders explicit tread ownership; its numerical gate passes but native ordinary walls remain plain with ragged turf at lower transitions. Tall surfaces retain upright support organization.

`volumes` uses finite Nature-derived body sections and fails upper-lip and silhouette checks. `bearing` limits inward underside slope and passes the focused gate, but its in-game appearance is still inadequate. `joined` removes unrelated small detail and is rejected from the tall render for fabric-like fluting.

`field` replaces separately rooted shelf supports with one broad body; this is flatter and loses two cap samples. `slope` restores partial supports. `rockfield`, `scaledbody`, and `reliefbody` vary the broad field and test height-aware depth compression; they regain lower depth but still read as a patterned wall with thin strips. `scaled` changes the geological section spacing on short walls; the game view is still plain and it is not selected. `naturalbody` adds finite authored rock profiles to the broad body; the tall view remains weak and the upper silhouette guard fails at 0.12244 m excess. These visual-only intermediate variants are not claimed to pass unrun checks.
