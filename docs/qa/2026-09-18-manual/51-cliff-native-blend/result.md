# Larger stone sections and gradual lower projection

The selected geometry is applied to production. It replaces small randomly patched wear with larger, shallower stone sections, restrains the whole upper profile rather than just the last metre, and retains longer curved shelves with supported lower shoulders. This is a scoped improvement following the owner’s latest direction, not final acceptance of every cliff or the original judging register.

## Construction

The broad stone shapes and their shallow boundary depressions share one warped geological partition. Its characteristic spacing is 7.3 m and its physical joint depression is 0.10 m. Additional shallow wear is associated with actual blended rock junctions and shelf risers. No independent fine crack patches are introduced. Tread interiors remain protected from perpendicular relief channels.

The body grows with descent and the final projection is bounded by the actual rooted depth over the wall’s height. Larger lower outcrops remain supported. A thin continuous stone face spans the periodic native backing away from attachment edges; native depth and normals are borrowed only near the real joins. The old normal blend was still imprinting backing tiles onto a face 0.25 m beyond their crests. The old height-proportional crown notch is bounded, and a depth multiplier that repeated the same lateral pattern at every height becomes two-dimensional.

Sparse major ledges coexist with short shelves, at distinct heights, lengths, thicknesses, grades and curvature. Nearby optional short cuts yield to a major shelf; the Nature-derived supporting lower terraces remain. Maximum authored span is 46 m. The fixed tall control has five connected upper turf spans from **4.75 to 40.75 m**, versus the earlier 3–9.25 m control. This does not mean every long ledge has uniform width or slope.

## Red-first evidence

The prior production collar exceeds the outer-silhouette gradual-growth bound by **2.687962 m**. The selected geometry has **zero excess** over 304,469 upper-profile samples across 8/16/32/64 m controls. The bound measures from the actual 1 m native crest with a 0.4 m allowance for shallow independent stone relief. It does not force every new face to follow the native tile’s inset valleys.

The earlier pass-50 valley-relative diagnostic is retained and still fails on this version at **0.694989 m**. Requiring every independent face to track individual mortar valleys would reintroduce the rejected periodic pattern. The replacement silhouette invariant is documented explicitly and fails on the actual prior production source; the historical test is not silently relaxed.

A flat face beyond the native crest inherits up to **49.304554 degrees** of spurious normal tilt on prior production. It now stays within **0.001237 degrees** of its geometric normal over 30 samples. The continuous-cover diagnostic fails at 80.20% coverage on the pass-50 taper experiment and reaches 100% here; prior production already passes that coverage check, so this is prevention of the taper experiment’s regression rather than a new baseline defect claim.

The broad-outline correlation improves from pass 50’s failing 0.682635 to **0.527707**, passing the unchanged 0.65 maximum. Long vertical stretches still exist in the tall composition, so that measurement is not universal art acceptance.

The shaped trial bounds the notches and carries the larger stone field through protruding masses. Its oblique and overhead tall views are closer to the owner-selected earlier tall reference.
