# Cliff projection and carved-ledge investigation

No candidate from this pass is promoted. Production remains pass 60: larger shallow geological sections, the thinner upper attachment skin, curved long ledges and locally rooted lower rocks. The native game comparisons still expose broad plain faces, and the final connected-turf trial introduces jagged grass boundaries. Passing geometry checks do not make that an acceptable art change.

## What was isolated

The previous root-derived upper bound clips different incoming rock depths to a common exterior slope. Multiplying their whole excess depth by descent preserves a continuous upper transition, but also compresses the actual shelves. Separately, cumulative ledge support retains the same lateral outline far below each ledge. Finite Nature Pack bodies alone do not resolve that supporting geometry.

The most useful construction experiment cuts part of a ledge into the stone above it and retains a smaller supporting shoulder below. Actual Nature Pack profiles have independent finite heights and lateral positions, rather than being stretched over the whole wall. This reduces the broad outline correlation in the pinned 48-by-64 m control from 0.493798 to 0.406359; both already pass the historical 0.65 diagnostic, so this is a comparison, not a newly reproduced red test or an art score.

An additional deformation issue was identified: the depth envelope changes a tread's width after its vertical curve has been constructed. Keeping its old vertical drop can make the final shelf too steep. Recomputing only the excessive drop against the final width restores some long turf spans in the carved candidate. Its connected upper spans increase from a 10 m maximum to 25 m; the combined inset/support version reaches 42.5 m, with six spans and a 2 m minimum. This correction remains a prototype.

## Variants and checks

- `blended`: multiply the excess body depth by descent rather than clipping it to a plane. Six of eight tests pass (13/16 assertions). The full cap has only 51/57 turf hits, and the longest qualifying upper shelf falls to 6.5 m. The native image is thin and plain. Rejected.
- `spread`: replace most of the persistent local support field with a progressively wider weighted neighboring field, while retaining the local cap jump. Eight tests / sixteen assertions pass. Tall upright organization and dark narrow features remain. Unselected.
- `sections`: use broad geological-cell relief as the main body as well as the shallow surface divisions. The native tall view becomes a strongly segmented cobblestone wall. Rejected visually without expanding the test run.
- `interlocked`: overlapping anisotropic rock volumes instead of depth returning to zero at each geological boundary. Eight tests / sixteen assertions pass, but the native yellow-world face remains slab-like and plain. Unselected.
- `native-volume`: finite source-derived Nature Pack bodies with independently placed centres and heights. The tall view still inherits strong support ribs. Unselected after native inspection.
- `carved`: finite Nature bodies plus shelves carved into the upper rock instead of fully additive supports. Seven of nine tests pass (15/17 assertions). Only 28/57 cap samples are turf and the maximum qualifying upper span is 10 m.
- `carved-caps`: limit each tread's vertical drop using its final projected width. Eight of nine tests pass (16/17 assertions). Maximum upper span becomes 25 m, but cap coverage stays 28/57.
- `inset`: retain a smaller lower supporting shoulder in addition to the upper cut. Eight of nine tests pass (16/17 assertions); cap coverage improves to 45/57. Six connected upper spans range from 2 to 42.5 m. Both upper-envelope checks, immediate lower bearing, shell closure, channel separation and the broad-outline diagnostic pass. Overall visual composition is still unaccepted.
- `inset-turf`: let very shallow upward rock triangles join seeded tread components. Cap coverage becomes 57/57, but the pointed-continuation guard finds one abrupt cutoff. Eight of nine tests pass (16/17 assertions).
- `inset-connected`: use the same upward-surface threshold throughout a connected seeded ledge. Nine tests / seventeen assertions pass, including 57/57 cap coverage. Native P12 and P05 images expose jagged patches on adjacent rock. Rejected despite the green run.

## The cap regression is understood, not waived

`cap-diagnostic.gd` traces all twelve missing inset hits to two shallow rock triangles adjoining the shelf. Their upward normal components are 0.970686 and 0.953775. They are physically part of the upward continuation but are emitted in the riser band, so the original material classification leaves them stone. Extending eligibility by slope closes those gaps, but introduces a new angular material boundary elsewhere. The final candidate is therefore not installed. The existing test thresholds and production tests are unchanged.

A future attempt needs coherent shelf-boundary geometry/material ownership through these adjoining surfaces. Another global slope cutoff or a disconnected painted patch would repeat the observed failure. Removing all bearing with a smooth maximum, adding more random fine cracks, or simply stretching the Nature bodies again does not address the underlying composition.
