# Settlement compositions from the existing assets

The reference suggests a warm gathering place with readable houses and a strong tree silhouette. Better compositions should give doors an address, keep the centre usable, group minor props beside buildings, and choose one dominant landmark rather than scattering every asset through every town.

| Composition | Arrangement and assets | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Well hamlet | 3–6 small native LPFV houses facing a shared square; `sfv.well.001` inside a clear walking loop | Implemented |
| Fire hamlet | Small native houses around `sfbp.campfire.001`, with open approaches and room for gathering | Implemented |
| Tree green | A reserved central green around `lpfv.tree.01`; houses face the green and stay outside the full native canopy footprint | Implemented |
| Ridge village | An elongated inhabited spine, lower flanks, platforms and native houses on shared ground streets | Implemented |
| Courtyard warren | Occupied mass around an intentional open court; prefab and modular frontage share circulation | Implemented |
| Crescent village | Unequal arms around an open ground notch, with a taller back and low approach | Implemented |
| Mill village | One large SFV windmill as the outer landmark, lower houses along its approach, open ground beneath the rotor sweep | Proposed; measure and bake the native assembly and moving clearance first |
| Watermill court | Existing watermill pieces beside a real downhill channel, with a waterside court above the hydraulic surface | Proposed; catalogue the pieces and derive bank support from the actual water/terrain plan |
| Island hamlet | 3–5 houses and one tree/well on an existing dry island; one shared landing and a bank-to-bank bridge | Proposed; select genuine dry ground enclosed by connected water, not a decorative water sheet |
| Twin villages | Two small groups with distinct squares linked by the existing native bridge across a real gap or channel | Proposed; solve both banks, bridge arch and walking handoffs as one compound |

The SFV windmill files are components/versions of one assembly, not three independent styles. The measured raw assembled bounds are about 50.33 × 73.06 × 42.88 source units. Those raw dimensions are not an approved runtime scale. The watermill inventory similarly contains parts, not six finished usable buildings.

For a later landmark pass, the strongest order is mill village, then paired river settlements, then island hamlets. Each adds a recognizable silhouette and a reason for the layout. One major landmark per small settlement, varied house heights and occasional empty frontage would keep those settlements readable. Seasonal gardens and small workshops can provide secondary variation without filling the public square.

T-shaped roofs now use compatible derivatives of the S002 slope asset. The existing S001 bisected pieces had a different pitch; merely inserting those pieces would leave another gap. Twelve native signatures (host length/offset, color and eave side) are verified with real triangles.
